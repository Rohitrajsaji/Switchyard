## M9 production approval boundary checkpoint

- Production flag configuration now has one policy boundary. `auth.Authorize` still denies every production write by default (application keys, `workctl`, experiment lifecycle and any other caller stay blocked); `auth.AuthorizeEnv` exposes the scope check without that denial, for the central change policy only. - Direct production edits accept only `flags.Exempt` changes: setting `killed` or lowering standalone rollout traffic with every other field unchanged (audited with `policy: emergency_exempt`). Creation, raised traffic, value/default/targeting edits, rollout removal and re-enablement are denied and leave no revision or audit row.
- Migration 0016 adds `proposals` with database-enforced immutable content, a fixed state graph (`validated` to `approved`/`rejected`/`stale`; `approved` to `applied`/`rejected`/`stale`/`expired`), final terminal states, approver different from proposer, approval-expiry checks, and no row removal. ## M9 rollout machinery, guardrails and rollback

- Migration 0017 adds `rollout_plans`, `rollout_steps`, append-only `guardrail_checks` and `safety_rollbacks`, with triggers for immutable plan content, a fixed state graph, final states and approver ≠ proposer, plus an inactive `system:rollout` attribution user. Plans drive a running experiment's eligible traffic through reviewed steps (≤10 points each, within a ceiling) in `scheduled` or `metric` mode; `flags.ApplyTraffic` changes only traffic, so salts, variants and weights stay frozen.
- Lock order is flag row then plan row everywhere (create, approve, start, cancel, tick, rollback). The worker (`WORKER_ROLLOUTS_ENABLED`, every 10 s) applies at most one step or one safety transition per plan per tick. Rollback is one transaction: killed revision, pending steps cancelled, evidence and cooldown recorded, audit as `system:rollout` with `emergency_exempt`. A manual traffic path (`POST .../experiments/{run}/traffic`) accepts ≤10-point raises outside production and decreases anywhere.
- Guardrails: sliding-window request facts per non-control variant (min requests, error rate, exact p95), two consecutive breaches by default, matured-cohort conversion check with a concurrent-control and backlog guard. Insufficient evidence neither promotes nor rolls back. After a rollback `proposals.Apply` refuses to re-enable the flag during the cooldown (the HTTP path maps it to 409 `cooldown`).
- Evidence: race-enabled PostgreSQL tests cover validation bounds, separation of duties, approval expiry at 24 h, one-live-plan-per-run, scheduled progression with eight concurrent ticks applying exactly one step, stale on pause, kill before/after progression in both orders, twelve randomized concurrent kill-versus-progression runs with no enabled revision after the first kill, metric promotion needing sufficient evidence, stale evidence aging out, a breach needing two consecutive checks before rollback with full evidence, healthy/small samples never rolling back, conversion cohort boundary fixtures (20% boundary, small cohorts, no control, backlog) and database guards. Two failures found on the first run (an exclusive window end excluded facts stamped at the check time) were fixed. The cooldown refusal inside `proposals.Apply` has no dedicated integration test because production experiments cannot yet produce a rollback fixture; only `ActiveCooldown` itself is tested.
- Live: `make rollout-drill` on the rebuilt Docker stack: a healthy treatment promoted 8000 → 9000 under an approved metric plan; after 1,000 broken treatment requests (30% errors, 900 ms) the safety rollback fired 20.2 s later (two 10 s checks), cancelled the remaining step, and evaluation returned the safe value 1.12 s after the rollback committed (single observation; evidence in `docs/benchmarks/m9-rollout-drill.txt`).
